# Phase 4: Momentum and measurement

## Context
[plan.md](plan.md). Interest decays in days; trust builds from responsiveness.

## Requirements
- **Response promise:** reply to every issue and Discussions thread within two days; label first-time-friendly issues
  `good first issue`; close or answer stale ones.
- **Release cadence:** short, clear release notes (what changed for the user, what to do if anything); one release in the
  week after launch that fixes the top reported problems and says so.
- **Feedback loop into the product:** every piece of feedback becomes an issue, a backlog row in
  `docs/plans/2026-10-10-observation-backlog.md`, or a polite "not now" with the reason.
- **Measure:** at day 7 and day 30 record in a report: stars, downloads per release asset, unique cloners and views
  (GitHub traffic page, owner-only), issues opened and closed, Discussions threads, listings live, and three direct quotes
  of how users describe the project.
- **Day-30 review:** compare with the targets in the plan; decide what to keep (channels that worked), drop and try next
  (a talk, a video walkthrough, a comparison page, a plugin or template that makes the first skill faster to add).

## Files
`plans/reports/promote-day7-<date>.md` and `promote-day30-<date>.md` (new); backlog rows when feedback needs work.

## Steps
1. Day 0 to 7: answer, fix, release once, thank early users publicly.
2. Day 7: first measurement and a short note on what surprised us.
3. Day 30: second measurement, decision on the next round, update this plan's status.

## Validation
Both reports exist, the numbers come from GitHub's own pages, and the day-30 review names at least one thing to stop and
one to double down on.

## Risk and rollback
None to the code. If the response promise cannot be kept, change the promise in the README rather than going silent.
